Driving test: six cameras, TensorRT Edge-LLM recipe

Alpamayo2-Edge is a driving model: from six cameras (22 frames), the vehicle's recent motion and a navigation instruction it writes a short chain of causation and plans six candidate trajectories. The table above measures only its vision-language part, served with vLLM in BF16 with greedy decoding, the way the model answers questions. Its answers to "Describe the scene." are short (2 to 48 tokens on average per cell), so its tok/s figures swing more than other rows'. This test runs the whole model with its official FP8 TensorRT Edge-LLM recipe (TensorRT Edge-LLM v0.11.1, 5 diffusion steps, 6 candidates, greedy decoding of the chain of causation) on the recipe's 19 navigation demo clips, 3 runs each.

Jetson AGX Thor at MAXN with jetson_clocks and TensorRT 11.3; the recipe's own figures come from a DRIVE AGX Thor with TensorRT 11.3. As in the recipe, each decision encodes only the newest frame of every camera and reuses the earlier ones.

With all 22 frames encoded inside the timed run a decision takes 402 ms. TensorRT 11.3 brought the trajectory expert from 99 to 70 ms; fixing the GPU and memory clocks with jetson_clocks (memory at 4266 MHz instead of about 2750) brought the rest.

The recipe gives about 7 ms per token for greedy decoding but no greedy total; about 320 ms is its stages plus 22 tokens × 7 ms. On the recipe's example clip the chain of causation matches the recipe's text word for word.
