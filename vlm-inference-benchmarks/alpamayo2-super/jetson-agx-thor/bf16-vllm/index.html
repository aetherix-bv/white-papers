Driving test: six cameras, official PyTorch inference

Alpamayo2-Super (a 32B Qwen3-VL backbone and a 2.3B diffusion expert) writes a short chain of causation from six cameras (4 frames each) and the vehicle's recent motion, then plans a trajectory. The table above measures only its vision-language part, served with vLLM in BF16. To "Describe the scene." that part mostly answers in a few tokens (2 to 34 on average per cell) that no scene description was provided, although it does describe the image when asked what it sees ("The scene shows a road intersection"); its tok/s figures therefore swing more than other rows'. This test runs the whole model with the official inference path of its repository (NVlabs/alpamayo2, inference_smoke.py: BF16, 10 diffusion steps, one trajectory, top-p 0.98, temperature 0.6) on the repository's 5 validation clips, 3 runs each, Jetson AGX Thor at MAXN.

In the table above 2 requests came back with an empty answer; as in the camera limit, an empty answer is not counted as a failure, so it does not end a series. There is no TensorRT recipe for this model; attention runs through PyTorch SDPA, because flash-attn has no build for this board.

Each decision reads 24 images (4,580 prompt tokens) and writes about 18 tokens of chain of causation (10 to 32); the longer the chain, the longer the decision. The model takes 69 GiB of memory. Alpamayo2-Edge, with its TensorRT recipe on the same board, takes 331 ms per decision.
